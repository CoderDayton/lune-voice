The onboarding flow needs a complete rethink. Right now, new users hit a landing page, then a sign-up form, then a verification email, then a profile setup wizard that's five steps long. By the time they actually get to use the product, they've spent three to four minutes just creating an account. The data backs this up. We're seeing a 40 percent drop-off between the sign-up form and the verification step, and another 25 percent drop-off during the profile wizard. That means we're losing over half our potential users before they even see the main dashboard. My proposal is to let people start using the product immediately with a temporary session. They can sign up later when they've already seen the value. For the profile information, we can collect it gradually through contextual prompts instead of a big upfront form. This is what Figma and Notion do, and their activation rates are significantly higher than ours. I'd like to put together a prototype next sprint and run an AB test with about 10 percent of new traffic. If the numbers look good, we can roll it out fully by end of quarter.
